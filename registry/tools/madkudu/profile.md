## The one job claimed, measure by measure

`sales.score-leads`. Every figure here is the vendor's own.

**Conversion lift of top decile: the vendor's figure, unmeasured here.** The [customer fit article](https://help.hginsights.com/docs/customer-fit-scoring) says: "Leads with a “very good” customer fit usually convert about 10 times more than leads with a “low” customer fit." That compares the top segment with the bottom one, not the top decile with arrival order, and the article does not say how large the top segment is, over which customers, or in which period. The vendor's [Chartio customer story](https://hginsights.com/resource/customer-stories/chartio-increases-product-trial-conversion-to-paying-customers-by-40/) says "MQTs are 5x more likely to convert to a customer", for one customer, with no counts. An HG Insights [blog post](https://hginsights.com/blog/the-hidden-flaw-in-most-b2b-lead-scoring-models/) (2026-08-10) describes a test at Gusto "with a real control group: high-scoring leads converted 15% better when a rep called, at 98% statistical confidence". That is the only figure I found with a control group. It measures what calling does within the high-scoring group, which is a different question from how much better the top of the list converts; it publishes no counts.

**Score calibration: empty.** The segments are labels, not probabilities, so there is nothing published to calibrate against. The product shows each customer a [model performance page](https://help.hginsights.com/docs/monitoring-your-model-performance), and its main number is recall: "The model performance recall is calculated by taking the sum of the ‘Very Good’ and ‘Good’ conversions and dividing that by all the conversions in the past 6 months (uncohorted)." Recall says how many conversions the top segments caught, not how often a lead in them converts, and the page introduces its own charts as illustrative, not a published result: "We will be including charts from a fake business along with some examples of insights that we can gather from these charts."
